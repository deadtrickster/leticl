  inline, against Rust's ~3 ns/byte baseline — a stored 4–6 MB session is 1.6–2.7 s of parse.

  Their three levers, smallest-first, as they wrote them:

  1. Lex only what is drawn — "bounds the work, changes the Block model".
  2. Cache the rendered history — "strongest for --continue, needs a disk format".
  3. Paint chrome first, fill over frames — "touches what makes scrollback correct".

  Lever 1 is our architecture. %viewport-lines walks the transcript backwards and stops as soon as it has the lines the viewport needs, so cost is bounded by the screen and not the session — which is exactly
  why our 2000-item attach is 0.29 s and theirs is measured in seconds. I documented that measurement in S10 earlier; this is it paying off.
